# O5 — arithmetic connected correlations

For distinct candidate offsets `m_1,...,m_k`, define the selected-centre tuple count

\[
T_X(m_1,\dots,m_k)
=\frac1N\sum_{j<N}
\prod_{a=1}^{k}
1_{\mathbb P}(P_j+m_a),
\]

with the candidate-prime conditions on the `m_a` retained separately or through von Mangoldt weights.

The raw factorial moment is the sum of `T_X` over distinct offset tuples. The factorial cumulant is obtained by Möbius inversion on set partitions:

\[
T_X^{\mathrm{conn}}(m_1,\dots,m_k)
=
\sum_{\pi\in\Pi_k}
(|\pi|-1)!(-1)^{|\pi|-1}
\prod_{B\in\pi}T_X((m_a)_{a\in B}).
\]

The programme must derive the exact normalization appropriate to the common row variable `j`; no probabilistic independence is assumed.

## Required cancellation order

Disconnected main terms must cancel in `T_X^conn` before estimates are applied. Taking absolute values at the raw moment level recreates the fixed-order obstruction and loses the intended compression.

## Local-factor audit

For each connected offset graph, separate:

1. primes `p<=ell_j` dividing `P_j`, for which all output forms share the primorial local bias;
2. primes `p>ell_j` dividing offset differences `m_a-m_b`;
3. collision primes producing fewer than `k` distinct output residue classes;
4. the tail Euler product.

The small-prime primorial enhancement belongs in the first cumulant/main term. It must not be counted repeatedly as a connected interaction.

## Candidate cluster norms

Test at least two norms:

\[
\mathcal C_k
=\sum_{m_1,\dots,m_k}^{\ast}
|T_X^{\mathrm{conn}}(m_1,\dots,m_k)|,
\]

and a weighted tree norm

\[
\mathcal T_k
=\sum_{m_1,\dots,m_k}^{\ast}
|T_X^{\mathrm{conn}}(m_1,\dots,m_k)|
\prod_{a=1}^{k}w_X(m_a).
\]

A viable criterion must make

\[
\sum_{k\ge2}\frac{q_X^k}{k!}\mathcal C_k
\]

or its signed/weighted analogue smaller than the first-order mass by more than `(1+epsilon) log N`.

## Dependency graph test

Construct the arithmetic graph on offsets with edges generated by shared collision primes or other exact local interactions. Determine whether a sparse graph or tree-graph inequality is legitimate after conditioning on the primorial base.

A dense graph is not by itself fatal; the programme must measure weighted interaction strength. However, no abstract cluster theorem may be invoked without verifying its convergence criterion numerically and symbolically at the registered scale.

## Permitted outcomes

- a proved connected remainder estimate;
- reduction to one explicit selected-centre connected-correlation theorem;
- an explicit divergence or norm obstruction closing this route.
