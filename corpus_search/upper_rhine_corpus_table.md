## Coverage gaps
- This corpus includes explicitly provisional candidates. Broad 15th-century, mid-century and third-quarter datings are retained when they overlap 1400–1455; they do not prove copying before 1455. The three-plus subset is strict about documented content categories, not about precise dating or certainty of regional attribution.
- Coverage is strongest for Alsace/Haguenau, Swabia, Lake Constance and broadly Alemannic or southwestern German production. No additional explicitly localized qualifying pre-1455 Vorarlberg or Schaffhausen witness was established. These are discovery gaps, not assertions of historical absence.
- Basel is underrepresented. Several medical compilations have only undated or later Carthusian ownership. A present Basel shelfmark, Council attendance or a scribe’s birthplace is not treated as proof of contemporary manuscript movement.
- Precisely localized and dated 1400–1420 and 1451–1455 witnesses remain sparse. Unit-level watermarks, palaeography and binding history need further examination, particularly in composites.
- Unillustrated Latin working books, medical glossaries, computus within theological miscellanies and practical additions to unrelated books remain incompletely indexed. Illustrated German books are disproportionately visible in the consulted catalogues.
- French-, Italian- and Latin-language descriptions were searched alongside German resources, but no separate securely qualifying French-, Italian- or Hebrew-language regional group was established.
- Some records explicitly identify indirect content evidence based on the documented structure of a complete textual version rather than a manuscript-level chapter inventory. These need image-level checking; a title alone is not treated as adequate evidence.
- Catalogue-only means no manuscript-image resource was verified, not proof that none exists. Partially digitised includes isolated reproductions and resources whose completeness could not be verified. Catalogue-only discoveries are retained separately from available facsimiles.
- Cod. Lichtenthal 31 is a geographic borderline candidate: composite metadata name the northern Upper Rhine, but allocation to the 15th-century medical/botanical unit remains unverified. Its inclusion is explicitly provisional.

## Unsuccessful search areas
- Targeted Schaffhausen and Vorarlberg searches did not establish an additional witness meeting all period, geography and content conditions.
- Constance- and Basel-Council person/movement searches did not establish an additional qualifying manuscript merely from a named individual’s activity or birthplace.
- Basel Carthusian medical-miscellany searches yielded near-misses with undated or later ownership, rather than verified early regional presence.
- French-language ARCA/IRHT, Gallica/Numistral and related catalogue searches returned useful records and near-misses, but no additional separate French-language regional class.
- Hebrew medical/astronomical leads and Italian-language catalogue searches did not establish another witness with documented period movement into the corridor.
- Incunable-linked miscellany searches, including Augsburg 4° Cod. 180, did not resolve the dating and assembly of potentially earlier manuscript portions.
- Late-stage searches of displaced collections, broad-date overlaps, herb-vocabulary witnesses and remaining illustrated medical descriptions mostly returned duplicates or non-qualifying/unresolved material. Failure to retrieve unit-level descriptions is not treated as proof of absence.

## Exhaustion assessment (agent's own)
The discovery corpus contains 42 distinct shelfmarks, including explicitly flagged uncertain-date and uncertain-geography candidates; 16 have evidence for three or more requested content categories. Access is classified as 26 fully digitised, 4 partially digitised and 12 catalogue-only. Discovery combined e-codices, e-manuscripta, Swisscollections, Handschriftencensus, KdiH, Handschriftenportal-linked descriptions, institutional digital libraries, ARCA/IRHT, older printed catalogue references and scholarly text-transmission resources. Mirrors were reconciled by shelfmark, and distinct shelfmarks remain separate. Later additions were not used to manufacture an early mixed-content witness. Follow-up searches addressed geographic gaps, unillustrated/Latin material, botanical glossaries, displaced codices, broad-date overlaps and incunable-bound working books; these increasingly produced duplicates or unresolved/non-qualifying leads. This suggests substantial saturation of readily searchable descriptions, not global exhaustion. No complete shelf-by-shelf or image-by-image census was achieved. Unindexed catalogues, poorly described working books, constituent-unit dating and early ownership records remain substantial sources of possible additions. The near-miss register includes unresolved eligibility as well as definitive exclusions and should be used for follow-up, not as proof that every listed witness can never qualify.
