# Literature comparator ledger

Date: 2026-08-07

This is a priority-screening ledger, not a substitute for MathSciNet/Zentralblatt and specialist review. Absence from the searches recorded here is never evidence of novelty by itself.

## C1 — gain-graph lift matrices and Smith forms

- D. Funk, I. Pivotto, D. Slilaty, *Matrix representations of frame and lifted-graphic matroids correspond to gain functions*, Journal of Combinatorial Theory B 153 (2022). DOI: 10.1016/j.jctb.2022.01.006.
  - For additive gains, the canonical lift matrix is an oriented graph incidence matrix plus one gain row. This is the exact ambient matrix architecture of Paper I, Theorem 4.1 when every edge gain is 1.
  - Consequence: the affine-rank/balanced-cycle portion is not safely marketable as a new framework. The integral invariant-factor refinement may still be new; this search did not locate an existing theorem giving the exact `1,...,1,g` Smith form for the constant-gain integral lift.
- J. Grossman, D. Kulkarni, I. Schochetman, *On the minors of an incidence matrix and its Smith normal form*, Linear Algebra and its Applications 218 (1995), 213–224. DOI: 10.1016/0024-3795(93)00173-W.
  - Complete SNF theory for ordinary undirected incidence matrices; not the same lifted matrix, but a mandatory comparator for any integral incidence-matrix novelty claim.
- Gain/lift-matroid rank theory originating with Zaslavsky and subsequent representation literature is a mandatory specialist search target.

## C2 — Sidon, representation functions, superincreasing sequences

- Classical Sidon/B2 literature treats uniqueness of pair sums and bounded representation functions.
- F. Benevides et al., *Additive properties of a pair of sequences*, Acta Arithmetica 139 (2009), 185–197, studies pair-sum representation functions and generalized Sidon conditions.
- Y. Jing, A. Mudgal, *Finding large additive and multiplicative Sidon sets in sets of integers*, Mathematische Annalen 391 (2025), 685–715, uses modern `B_h^+[g]` language for bounded additive representation counts.
- Superincreasing sequences have unique bounded subset-sum representations by elementary greedy/dominance arguments. Therefore Paper III's mechanism is not a new general method; the exact two-level multiplicity law for the primorial-prefix pair-sum multiset is the possible new specialization.

## C3 — character moments and random multiplicative structures

- Character-sum moment and orthogonality arguments, including sixth moments, are classical and extensive; Paper IV's Lemma 5.2 is therefore not a standalone novelty claim.
- Nearby literatures include random multiplicative functions, short character sums, and character sums over permutation-generated sequences. The screen found no theorem matching Paper IV's exact random ordering of prime factors and reciprocal-frame path average.
- Consequently the potential novelty is the random primorial-product path theorem and its ordered-partition/path-matching assembly, not moment orthogonality itself.

## C4 — finite-field factorisation statistics and cohomological trace formulae

- T. Hyde, *Factorization statistics and the twisted Grothendieck–Lefschetz formula*, arXiv:1712.00071 (2017).
- D. Petersen, P. Tosteson, *Factorization statistics and bug-eyed configuration spaces*, Geometry & Topology 25 (2021), 3691–3723; arXiv:2004.06024.
- A. Entin, *Factorization statistics of restricted polynomial specializations over large finite fields*, Israel Journal of Mathematics 242 (2021), 37–53.
- W. Sawin, the factorisation-function square-root-cancellation theorem already cited by Paper V.

These establish that translating polynomial factorisation statistics into symmetric-group class functions and cohomological traces is mature machinery. Papers V–VI can claim novelty only for the sparse surface/cone, exact crown/q-line projectors, particular trace identities, or new quotient geometry—not for the general trace formalism.

## C5 — Hattori–Stallings traces and modular projectives

Hattori–Stallings traces/ranks are classical invariants of finitely generated projective modules and group rings. Relevant general literature includes work surveyed by Chatterji–Mislin and character/rank formulations for projective lattices. Paper VI's use of Hattori–Stallings trace is therefore established technology. The potentially new statement is the particular coefficient extraction/no-divided-hook consequence for the module arising from this sparse family.

## C6 — Artin–Schreier and Kummer geometry

Artin–Schreier cyclic degree-p torsors and Kummer forms are standard. The search found broad Artin–Schreier/Kummer quotient literature but no result matching Paper VI's exact sparse root-cycle quotient, common two-form count, unique projective fixed point, or compactified point-count identity. These are provisionally object-specific specializations, not new general quotient theory.

## C7 — bilateral endpoint/Frobenius incidence

Searches by the underlying objects—four distinct irreducible polynomial moduli, scalar reciprocal congruences, `X^q-X`, common defect, reflection/translation families, and all-odd-characteristic quadratic emptiness—did not locate a direct prior analogue. Returned literature was generic finite-field incidence and irreducible-polynomial/Frobenius work.

This is the strongest provisional novelty signal in the corpus, but the bibliography is thin enough that `NEW` is not certified. Paper VII's defect/classification/quadratic theorem remains `NEW_SPECIALIZATION` pending arithmetic-geometry specialist review.

## C8 — finite Bonferroni and factorial-moment criteria

Finite Bonferroni inequalities, inclusion–exclusion, factorial moments and moment-to-tail optimization are standard. Their application to the selected-centre detector can be a useful exact reduction but should not be marketed as new general probability/combinatorics machinery.

## Screening conclusion

The literature screen materially changes the safe wording of the corpus:

- **framework novelty must be reduced** for gain-graph rank, Sidon/superincreasing uniqueness, character moments, Grothendieck–Lefschetz factorisation statistics, Hattori–Stallings traces, and Artin–Schreier/Kummer theory;
- **object-specific exact theorems remain plausible novelty** in the collision transport, exact integral Smith refinement, selected-centre obstructions, random primorial-product path, sparse finite-field geometry, quotient counts, and Paper VII incidence classification;
- no priority-sensitive theorem should carry an unqualified `new` claim until a specialist literature audit is complete.
