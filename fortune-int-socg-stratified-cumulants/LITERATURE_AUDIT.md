# Literature audit

## Montgomery and Soundararajan — *Primes in Short Intervals* (2004)

They derive, under strong Hardy--Littlewood input, Gaussian moment predictions for prime counts in ordinary moving short intervals in a power-length regime.

**Permitted use:** connected-moment anatomy, partition cancellation and conditional benchmarks.  
**Not supplied:** logarithmic-square windows at exponentially large primorial centres, sparse selected-centre averaging, or a one-defect error.

## Pintz — *On the singular series in the prime k-tuple conjecture* (2010)

Pintz strengthens Gallagher-type average singular-series estimates with uniformity as tuple size grows when the shift interval is sufficiently large relative to the tuple order.

**Permitted use:** C4 local-factor averages and conditional constants.  
**Not supplied:** prime-tuple incidence at the selected primorial rows.

## Kuperberg — *Sums of singular series with large sets and the tail of the distribution of primes* (2022)

Kuperberg controls singular-series sums for growing sets and derives prime-tail consequences under suitable uniform Hardy--Littlewood hypotheses.

**Permitted use:** quantify admissible tuple order and local-factor tails.  
**Not supplied:** the selected-centre Hardy--Littlewood theorem or ordinary-cumulant growth on the primorial walk.

## Matomäki and Radziwiłł — *Multiplicative functions in short intervals* (2016)

Their almost-all short-interval technology gives cancellation for broad multiplicative functions in almost all ordinary intervals.

**Permitted use:** source-decomposition comparison and falsification of naive transfer claims.  
**Not supplied:** primes, every selected primorial centre, or an absolute one-defect theorem.

## Guth and Maynard — *New large value estimates for Dirichlet polynomials* (2026)

Their large-value estimates yield improved zero-density results and primes in intervals of length `x^(17/30+o(1))`.

**Permitted use:** possible C7 large-value technology after an exact source reduction.  
**Not supplied:** polylogarithmic windows, primorial-selected centres, or connected cumulants.

## Jha — *The Poisson Tail Conjecture for Primes in Short Intervals* (2026)

Jha establishes conditional tail asymptotics for slowly growing parameters using a strong Hardy--Littlewood hypothesis and concentration tools.

**Permitted use:** C8 conditional tail budget.  
**Not supplied:** unconditional selected-centre input.

## Governing bridge test

An external theorem is admitted only if all of the following are verified:

1. its averaging variable is the registered primorial-row index;
2. its window includes `H=eta X^2` at centre size `P_j`;
3. tuple/cumulant order reaches the programme's growing range;
4. the error is below the absolute one-stratum budget;
5. the output concerns actual primes after all source recombination;
6. the conclusion is uniform for every registered stratum.
