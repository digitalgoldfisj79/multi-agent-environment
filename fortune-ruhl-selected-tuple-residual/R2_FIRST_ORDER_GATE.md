# R2 — absolute RUHL contains a constant-error mean theorem

Let

\[
\mathcal A_{b,K}
=
\sum_{k=0}^{K}\frac{q_b^k}{k!}|E_{b,k}|.
\]

Since every term is nonnegative,

\[
q_b|E_{b,1}|\le \mathcal A_{b,K}.
\]

Thus the sharp sufficient absolute condition `mathcal A_{b,K}<Delta_b` forces

\[
\boxed{
|E_{b,1}|<\frac{\Delta_b}{q_b}.
}
\]

Under the frozen geometry

\[
M_b\asymp\frac{X}{\log X},
\qquad
L_b\asymp X,
\qquad
q_b\asymp\frac{\log X}{X},
\qquad
\Delta_b\asymp\frac{\log X}{X},
\]

so

\[
\frac{\Delta_b}{q_b}=O(1).
\]

In the normalized registered panel `L_b=X`, `epsilon=0.10`, `rho_b=1.10`, `beta=5`, the exact allowance rises from `0.688` at `X=100` to about `0.916` at `X=10^6`; it remains bounded below one throughout the registered panel.

The mean itself is of order `X`. Therefore termwise absolute RUHL requires the deterministic selected-centre model to predict the average prime-pair occupancy with additive constant error, not merely with a positive linear lower bound. This is strictly stronger than the frozen `INT-SCME` requirement.

Consequently the absolute RUHL route does not bypass the selected-centre mean wall. It strengthens it at the first moment before any higher tuple order is considered.
