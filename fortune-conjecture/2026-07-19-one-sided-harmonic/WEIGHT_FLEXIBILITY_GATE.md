# Weight-flexibility and Fourier-scale gate

To obtain \(A\) effective harmonics at the mandatory critical modulus \(q\asymp H\), one must shorten the physical window to \(h\asymp H/A\). Covering the original length-\(H\) interval then requires \(A\) translates. For exact interval indicators,

\[
\sum_{b=0}^{A-1}\sum_{m=bh}^{(b+1)h-1}e(am/q)
=
\sum_{m=0}^{H-1}e(am/q).
\]

Thus the broad transform of one short window, multiplied by its translation Dirichlet factor, reconstructs the original length-\(H\) transform. This was checked in 1,722 finite systems with maximum error \(1.31\times10^{-13}\). Estimating translates separately pays the same factor \(A\).

Alternatively take \(Q=BH\). The low-frequency shell output has scale

\[
\frac{Q}{\log Q}=\frac{BH}{\log(BH)},
\]

so relative to \(H\) it costs \(B/\log(BH)\), while the pair conductor grows by \(B^2\). Polynomially growing \(B\) is inadmissible. More importantly, larger shells do not remove the compulsory \(Q\asymp H\) shell from the exact detector.

\[
\boxed{
\text{No admissible weight change creates a growing independent harmonic average at the critical shell.}
}
\]
