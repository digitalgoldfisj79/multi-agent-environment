# Top-level resonance and divisor-pinning gate

If

\[
K_X(\theta)=|H_2(\theta)|^2-M\ge M^{2-\delta},
\]

then, using \(2|H_2|\le |F|^2+N\) and \(M\asymp N^2\),

\[
|F(\theta)|\gg N^{1-\delta/2}.
\]

For fixed \(\delta>0\), the normalized resultant \(|F|/N\gg N^{-\delta/2}\) tends to zero. This permits diffuse bias rather than near-total phase alignment.

If unit phases have resultant \(\mu N\), then for \(0<\tau<\mu\), at least

\[
\frac{(\mu-\tau)N}{1-\tau}
\]

have projection at least \(\tau\) along the resultant. Taking \(\tau=\mu/2\) guarantees only \(\asymp N^{1-\delta/2}\) phases in an arc whose half-width tends to \(\pi/2\), not a narrow modular arc.

Even hypothetical constraints \(\|\theta L_i\|\le\varepsilon\), with \(\theta=a(r-q)/(qr)\), yield

\[
|n_1L_2-n_2L_1|\le\varepsilon(|L_1|+|L_2|).
\]

Since \(|L_i|\) is primorial-sized, forcing the integer on the left to vanish requires exponentially small \(\varepsilon\), unavailable from a power-scale large-value estimate.

Fresh weighted reciprocal-pair diagnostics found diffuse high values: among the largest samples, the median fraction of phases within 0.1 radians of the resultant was 5.45% at \(X=350\) and 5.15% at \(X=700\); the median number of consecutive phase increments within 0.01 radians of zero was 0 at both scales. These are diagnostics, not asymptotic evidence.
