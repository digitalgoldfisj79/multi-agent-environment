# Targeted novelty audit

## Scope and limitation

This was a theorem-specific search, not proof that no predecessor exists. Searches covered random permutations and partial products, character sums over products of primes, lacunary and dissociated trigonometric sums, Sidon/difference sets, and sampling without replacement. Consensus search was unavailable because the connected account had exhausted its monthly query allowance; the audit therefore used publisher pages, arXiv records, journal metadata, and standard references.

## Comparison table

| Component | Nearest established literature | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Superincreasing rigidity | Dissociated sets, Hadamard-gap sequences, uniqueness of bounded digit expansions | Standard mechanism. |
| Pair sums are distinct | Sidon/B2-set theory | Standard consequence of strong lacunarity. |
| Difference multiplicity is exactly `N` or `1` | Sidon difference representation and additive-energy literature | The exact two-level law for the unordered pair-sum set was not located verbatim. It is elementary and should be a structural lemma, not the headline breakthrough. |
| High moments and sub-Weibull tail | Rudin gap-series inequalities; Salem–Zygmund Gaussian theory; modern lacunary large/moderate deviations | Broad phenomenon is known. The explicit pair-sum polynomial, uniform top-level range, and constants are specialised refinements. |
| Uniform random ordering of block primes | Random permutations in finite groups | Related source of permutation entropy, but different random objects and observables. No result was found on Dirichlet-character evaluation of nested prime-prefix products with four-copy reciprocal energy. |
| Character sums over products of primes | Matomäki–Teräväinen; Szabó; multiplicative transference literature | These study representability of residue classes by bounded products, not products of random prefix cells or reciprocal pair-sum energy. |
| Random character sums | Harper and the Davenport–Erdős/Lamzouri tradition | Randomness is usually in interval location, character, or coefficients, not a without-replacement ordering of the prime factors. |
| Exceptional-character count | Standard moment orthogonality and unique factorisation | The ingredient is standard; its placement in the matching/ledger proof is the distinctive mechanism. |
| Final `M(log X)^9` theorem | No exact match found | Best current novelty candidate: rank conditioning, ratio-character decay, exceptional-character counting, and configuration closure at critical length. |

## Closest references

1. Salem and Zygmund (1947, 1948): classical Gaussian behaviour of lacunary trigonometric series.
2. Rudin (1960): `Lambda(p)` bounds for trigonometric series with gaps.
3. Aistleitner et al. and Prochno–Strzelecka: large and moderate deviations for lacunary sums, including arithmetic sensitivity.
4. Berend–Mamana (2021): statistics of uniformly random permutations of finite groups.
5. Müyesser–Pokrovskiy (2025): random-permutation methods for group product problems.
6. Matomäki–Teräväinen (2024): character-sum transference for products of primes in residue classes.
7. Harper (2025): probabilistic behaviour of character sums over random moving intervals.

## Cold novelty conclusion

The pair-sum moment material is unlikely to be the main source of field impact because classical lacunary theory predicts much of the behaviour. The random-order reciprocal-energy theorem is the credible novelty centre. Its ingredients are individually recognisable, but the exact theorem, critical scaling, and proof assembly were not located in the searched literature.

Recommended language:

> We prove a reciprocal-energy estimate for pair sums of a uniformly permuted prime-prefix product path. The proof combines exact ordered-set-partition conditioning with ratio-character decay and a sixth-moment count of exceptional characters.

Avoid until specialists confirm it:

> This is the first theorem of its kind; a new general method; or a major advance on Fortune's conjecture.
