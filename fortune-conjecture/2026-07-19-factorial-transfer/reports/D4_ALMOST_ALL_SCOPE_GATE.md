# D4 — almost-all-n scope gate

The conceptual move remains attractive: averaging over the primorial index could create a new variable absent from fixed-shell PGD2. However, the proposed finite test over \(X=350,700,1200\) is not yet mathematically load-bearing.

## Problems with the proposed test

1. No deterministic inequality has been stated that converts an average of shell residuals into a bound for the number of exceptional primorial indices.
2. The frames are neither independent replicates nor a canonical nested martingale. Their endpoint blocks overlap non-uniformly: the 700 and 1200 blocks share 26 prime endpoints (351 induced pair sums), while the other tested pairs share none.
3. A threshold of two null standard deviations across three shells would be a diagnostic, not evidence for an asymptotic density-one theorem.
4. The normalizations and pair spaces vary with \(X\), so summing scalar residuals does not automatically create cross-shell cancellation in a common Hilbert space.

## Required formulation before compute

Index the objects by the actual primorial index \(n\), define a nonnegative failure majorant \(B_n\), and prove an implication of the form
\[
\#\{n\le Y:\text{Fortune fails at }n\}
\le C\sum_{n\le Y}B_n.
\]
Then identify an averaged dispersion theorem proving \(\sum_{n\le Y}B_n=o(Y)\). Only after this bridge is fixed does a cross-shell numerical panel have a falsifiable meaning.

## Decision

Almost-all-n Fortune remains a potentially high-value scope move, but the three-shell experiment is stopped before execution because its implication gate is absent.
