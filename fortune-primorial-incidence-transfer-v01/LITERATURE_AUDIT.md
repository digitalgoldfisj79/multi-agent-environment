# Literature and internal-prior-art audit

Date: 27 August 2026
Novelty disposition: `PROVISIONAL_NEW_SPECIALIZATION_HUMAN_PRIORITY_REVIEW_REQUIRED`

## Candidate result being screened

For the deterministic cumulative product of all primes `q_i in [X,2X]`, reduced modulo an auxiliary prime `varpi>2X`, the candidate theorem proves

`|{B_X, B_X q_1, ..., B_X q_1...q_N} mod varpi| >> (X/(log X)^2)^(8/15)`.

For a prime `varpi in (2X,4X)`, this becomes

`>> varpi^(8/15)/(log varpi)^(16/15)`, beyond the square-root scale.

## 1. Direct method source — Hu 2026

Xiyu Hu, *Factorial residues modulo a prime: beyond the square-root bound*, arXiv:2608.01781 (3 August 2026), proves `|{k! mod p}| >> p^(8/15)`.

The present proof is explicitly an adaptation of Hu's mechanism:

1. a three-term multiplicative recurrence is rewritten as a fractional-linear map;
2. pairwise compositions linearise to affine lines;
3. nonidentity lines have multiplicity at most two;
4. Cauchy--Schwarz plus the Stevens--de Zeeuw Cartesian-product incidence theorem gives exponent `8/15`.

The new arithmetic input is that consecutive prime prefixes obey

`P_{j+2}=g_j P_{j+1}+P_{j+1}^2/P_j`,

where `g_j` is the next consecutive-prime gap, and that a single gap value repeats `>>X/(log X)^2` times in every sufficiently large dyadic block by PNT plus pigeonhole.

Accordingly, novelty—if any—is the primorial-prefix specialization and repeated-gap transfer, not the incidence mechanism or exponent extraction.

## 2. Fixed-polynomial products — Klurman--Munsch 2018/2020

Oleksiy Klurman and Marc Munsch, *Polynomial products modulo primes and applications*, arXiv:1810.06310, study value sets of cumulative products generated by values of a fixed polynomial and include the factorial case. Their framework is directly adjacent to modular cumulative-product value sets.

No result located in that paper or its search footprint treats the generators as the consecutive primes in a growing dyadic block. Consecutive primes are not values of a fixed polynomial, and the repeated-gap fractional-linear incidence structure used here is different.

Classification: `ADJACENT_FRAMEWORK_NOT_DIRECT_COLLISION`.

## 3. Factorial-residue literature before Hu

Banks--Luca--Shparlinski--Stichtenoth and subsequent factorial-residue papers establish square-root-scale and related value-set/product-set bounds for `n! mod p`; recent work before Hu gives approximately `sqrt(2p)` distinct factorial residues. These are essential method ancestry but do not contain the prime-prefix specialization located in the present searches.

Classification: `METHOD_ANCESTRY`.

## 4. Products of freely chosen primes modulo a modulus

Friedlander--Kurlberg--Shparlinski, Walker, Shparlinski, and Matomaki--Teravainen study which residue classes can be represented by products of two, three, or boundedly many freely selected primes. Those results concern product sets with independently chosen factors and do not preserve the deterministic cumulative-prefix ordering.

Classification: `DISTINCT_OBJECT`.

## 5. Consecutive-prime residue patterns

The literature on biases and patterns of consecutive primes modulo a fixed modulus studies the residue sequence of the primes `q_j` themselves. It does not, in the material located, give pointwise lower bounds for the value set of cumulative products `q_1...q_j` modulo an auxiliary prime.

Classification: `DISTINCT_OBJECT`.

## 6. Internal Fortune prior art

Standalone Paper I already studies the same cumulative-product path modulo primes, chiefly at modulus scale `r ~ X^2`. In particular it proves an **average almost-injectivity** result: averaged over a dyadic family of moduli, the support deficit of the prefix residues is `O(1/log X)` in relative terms, with an exceptional-modulus estimate.

That result is stronger on typical support size but is averaged over moduli and aimed at the `X^2` Fortune geometry. The new incidence theorem is **pointwise for every prime modulus `varpi>2X`**, and its beyond-square-root corollary lives at the much smaller comparable scale `varpi ~ X`.

Therefore the new theorem is not subsumed by Paper I's existing average almost-injectivity statement. It is complementary rather than a replacement.

Classification: `INTERNAL_NONCOLLISION_COMPLEMENTARY_REGIME`.
