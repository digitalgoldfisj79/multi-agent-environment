# General weight-zero collapse for the d=1 hook ledger

**Date:** 2026-07-22  
**Status:** exact theorem, conditional only on the already committed and independently audited geometric monodromy and local-inertia theorems. This proves the general-\(p\) weight-zero collapse lemma named in `D1_PUSH.md`. It does **not** prove the weight-one collapse or the function-field crown.

## 1. Setup

Let \(p\ge 5\) be prime and let \(q\in\mathbf F_p^\times\setminus\{2\}\). Consider the normalized root cover
\[
P_{q,t}(z)=qz^p+z^3-3z-(q-2)t=0
\]
over
\[
U=\mathbf P^1_t\setminus\{1,-1,\infty\}.
\]

Let \(\mathcal V\) be the rank-\(p-1\) standard local system obtained from the permutation local system on the \(p\) roots, and put
\[
\mathcal V_i=\bigwedge^i\mathcal V,\qquad 0\le i\le p-1.
\]

The geometric monodromy is \(S_p\). At \(t=\pm1\) geometric inertia is generated by a transposition. At infinity,
\[
I_\infty=C_p\rtimes C_{(p-1)/2},
\]
with its natural affine action on \(\mathbf F_p\). These inputs were proved and independently audited in the existing package.

Write
\[
W_{0,i}=\operatorname{Gr}^{W}_{0}H_c^1(U_{\overline{\mathbf F}_p},\mathcal V_i).
\]

Let \(u_q\in\mathbf F_p^\times/\mathbf F_p^{\times2}\) be the constant square class in
\[
\operatorname{Disc}_z P_{q,t}=u_q(t^2-1)
\quad\text{in }\mathbf F_p(t)^\times/\mathbf F_p(t)^{\times2}.
\]
Let \(\kappa_q\) be the one-dimensional quadratic Frobenius module with
\[
\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}_p^n\mid\kappa_q)=\chi(u_q)^n.
\]

## 2. Boundary description of weight zero

Let \(j:U\hookrightarrow\mathbf P^1\). For every finite-monodromy pure weight-zero local system \(\mathcal L\), there is a short exact sequence of sheaves
\[
0\longrightarrow j_!\mathcal L
\longrightarrow j_*\mathcal L
\longrightarrow
\bigoplus_{x\in\{1,-1,\infty\}}i_{x*}\mathcal L^{I_x}
\longrightarrow0.
\]

Since \(H_c^0(U,\mathcal L)=0\), this gives
\[
0\longrightarrow \mathcal L^{\pi_1(U_{\bar{\mathbf F}_p})}
\longrightarrow \bigoplus_x\mathcal L^{I_x}
\longrightarrow H_c^1(U_{\bar{\mathbf F}_p},\mathcal L)
\longrightarrow H^1(\mathbf P^1_{\bar{\mathbf F}_p},j_*\mathcal L)
\longrightarrow0.
\]

The last term is pure of weight one. In this finite-monodromy setting this can be checked directly: pass to the finite Galois cover that trivializes \(\mathcal L\), normalize \(\mathbf P^1\) in that cover, and take its smooth projective compactification \(\overline Y\). Then \(H^1(\mathbf P^1,j_*\mathcal L)\) is the relevant isotypic direct summand of \(H^1(\overline Y,\mathbf Q_\ell)\), which is pure of weight one. Therefore, in the Grothendieck group of semisimple arithmetic Frobenius modules,
\[
[W_{0,i}]
=
\sum_x[(V_i)^{I_x}]-[(V_i)^{S_p}],
\tag{2.1}
\]
where \(V_i=\bigwedge^i\operatorname{Std}_p\).

Thus the required alternating weight-zero class is determined entirely by local inertia invariants:
\[
\mathcal W_0
:=
\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}(-1)^i[W_{0,i}]
=
\sum_x\sum_i(-1)^i[(V_i)^{I_x}]
-\sum_i(-1)^i[(V_i)^{S_p}].
\tag{2.2}
\]

## 3. Local coset formula

Let \(I_x\) be a finite geometric inertia image and let \(\Phi_x\) be an arithmetic Frobenius lift in the decomposition group. Projection to \(I_x\)-invariants gives, for every \(n\ge1\),
\[
\sum_i(-1)^i
\operatorname{Tr}\!\left(\Phi_x^n\mid(V_i)^{I_x}\right)
=
\frac1{|I_x|}
\sum_{g\in I_x}
\det(1-\Phi_x^n g\mid\operatorname{Std}_p).
\tag{3.1}
\]

For every \(\sigma\in S_p\),
\[
\det(1-\sigma\mid\operatorname{Std}_p)
=
p\,\mathbf1_{\{\sigma\text{ is a }p\text{-cycle}\}}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Hence the local alternating trace is
\[
A_x(n)
=
\frac p{|I_x|}
\#\{g\in I_x:\Phi_x^n g\text{ is a }p\text{-cycle}\}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

This converts the entire weight-zero problem into a finite coset count.

## 4. The finite branch points contribute zero

At \(x=1\) and \(x=-1\), \(I_x=\langle\tau\rangle\) with \(\tau\) a transposition on the two colliding sheets.

The decomposition group normalizes \(\langle\tau\rangle\), hence preserves the support of \(\tau\), a two-element block. Therefore every element of every coset
\[
\Phi_x^n I_x=\{\Phi_x^n,\Phi_x^n\tau\}
\]
preserves that two-element block and its complement. Since \(p\ge5\), no such permutation is a \(p\)-cycle. Thus
\[
A_{1}(n)=A_{-1}(n)=0
\qquad(n\ge1).
\tag{4.1}
\]

This proves, uniformly in \(p\) and \(q\), the cancellation of all weight-zero constituents arising from the two finite critical fibres.

## 5. The infinity contribution

The group
\[
I_\infty=C_p\rtimes C_{(p-1)/2}
\]
is the index-two subgroup of \(\operatorname{AGL}_1(\mathbf F_p)\) consisting of affine maps
\[
x\longmapsto ax+b
\]
with \(a\) a square.

The arithmetic decomposition group normalizes the unique Sylow \(p\)-subgroup \(C_p\). Hence it lies in
\[
N_{S_p}(C_p)=\operatorname{AGL}_1(\mathbf F_p).
\]
Consequently the arithmetic Frobenius coset is either \(I_\infty\) or the nonsquare-multiplier coset.

The only \(p\)-cycles in \(\operatorname{AGL}_1(\mathbf F_p)\) are the nonidentity translations
\[
x\longmapsto x+b,\qquad b\ne0.
\]
There are exactly \(p-1\) of them, and all lie in \(I_\infty\). Therefore
\[
A_\infty(n)
=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{p(p-1)}{p(p-1)/2}=2,&\Phi_\infty^n\in I_\infty,\\[6pt]
0,&\Phi_\infty^n\notin I_\infty.
\end{cases}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Let \(\epsilon_q\in\{\pm1\}\) be the quadratic character of the quotient
\[
D_\infty/I_\infty\subseteq
\operatorname{AGL}_1(\mathbf F_p)/I_\infty.
\]
Then
\[
A_\infty(n)=1+\epsilon_q^n.
\tag{5.2}
\]

The geometric inertia is contained in \(A_p\). The quotient character is therefore the arithmetic sign character of the root permutation at infinity, which is the discriminant unit character. Hence
\[
\epsilon_q=\chi(u_q).
\tag{5.3}
\]

This also explains the arithmetic-versus-geometric quadratic noted in the independent soft-spot audit: it is not an error term but exactly the surviving weight-zero class.

## 6. Subtracting global invariants

Because the geometric monodromy is \(S_p\),
\[
(V_i)^{S_p}=0\quad(i>0),\qquad (V_0)^{S_p}=\mathbf Q_\ell.
\]
Thus the alternating global-invariant trace is \(1\).

Combining (4.1), (5.2), and (2.2), for every \(n\ge1\),
\[
\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Frob}_p^n\mid\mathcal W_0)
=
0+0+(1+\chi(u_q)^n)-1
=
\chi(u_q)^n.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Equality of all Frobenius power traces implies equality after semisimplification.

## 7. Theorem

### Theorem W0C

For every prime \(p\ge5\) and every \(q\in\mathbf F_p^\times\setminus\{2\}\),
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}(-1)^i
\left[
\operatorname{Gr}^{W}_{0}
H_c^1(U_{\overline{\mathbf F}_p},\mathcal V_i)
\right]
=
[\kappa_q].
}
\]

Equivalently, after even/odd semisimplification, every weight-zero Frobenius constituent cancels except one copy of the quadratic Kummer class associated with
\[
\chi\!\left(u_q(t^2-1)\right).
\]

This proves the general-\(p\) weight-zero collapse lemma named as the highest-value target in `D1_PUSH.md`.

## 8. Consequences and limits

What is now exact:

- all finite-puncture permutation and cyclotomic factors cancel in the even/odd weight-zero ledger for every \(p\);
- the sole survivor is the arithmetic quadratic at infinity;
- the previously flagged unramified quadratic discrepancy is precisely required by the theorem;
- the \(p=5\) and \(p=7\) spectra are instances of a uniform local coset identity.

What remains open:

- cancellation and geometric identification of the weight-one constituents;
- completeness of the proposed \(B_q,D_q,\ldots\) survivor list for general \(p\);
- the \(q\)-line surface-cohomology constant needed for positivity;
- the function-field \(d=1\) crown.
