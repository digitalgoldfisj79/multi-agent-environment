# Cyclic cubic Milnor trace collapse in the tame lift

**Date:** 2026-07-23  
**Status:** exact algebraic theorem over characteristic zero, or over any field of characteristic prime to `6p`, for every odd prime `p>=5`. It computes the cyclic trace in the tame lifted model that is expected to represent the post-Artin–Schreier corner. Identification of that model with the actual primitive corner complex remains a separate specialization theorem.

## 1. Standard hyperplane and cubic

Let `k` be a field with `char(k)` prime to `6p`. Put

`H={ (x_0,...,x_(p-1)) in A^p : sum_i x_i=0 }`.

The cyclic group `C_p=<sigma>` acts by

`sigma(x_0,...,x_(p-1))=(x_1,...,x_(p-1),x_0)`.

Consider the invariant cubic

`f=sum_i x_i^3 | H`.

The vector space underlying `H` is the standard representation `Std_p`.

## 2. The singularity is isolated

A constrained critical point satisfies

`3x_i^2=lambda`

for every `i`. Thus, after adjoining a square root if necessary, every `x_i` is `+r` or `-r`. The equation `sum_i x_i=0` gives

`(#plus-#minus)r=0`.

Because `p` is odd, `#plus-#minus` is an odd nonzero integer of absolute value at most `p`; in the stated characteristic it is nonzero. Hence `r=0` and the origin is the only critical point.

The Jacobian ideal is generated by `p-1` homogeneous quadrics forming a regular sequence. Consequently the Milnor algebra

`M_f=Sym(H^*)/(partial f)`

is a graded complete intersection with ordinary Hilbert series

`Hilb(M_f,t)=(1+t)^(p-1)`

and Milnor number

`mu(f)=2^(p-1)`.

## 3. Equivariant complete-intersection trace

The gradient equations transform as the same `C_p`-module `H^*`. Therefore the graded character of an element `g in C_p` on the Milnor algebra is

`Tr(g|M_f;t)=det(1-t^2 g|H^*)/det(1-t g|H^*)`.

For a nonidentity power `g=sigma^k`, the eigenvalues on `H` are all nontrivial p-th roots of unity. Since multiplication by `k` permutes them,

`det(1-tg|H)=product_(j=1)^(p-1)(1-t zeta_p^j)`

`             =(1-t^p)/(1-t)`,

while, because `2` is invertible modulo `p`,

`det(1-t^2g|H)=product_(j=1)^(p-1)(1-t^2 zeta_p^j)`

`               =(1-t^(2p))/(1-t^2)`.

Hence

### Theorem CCM.1 — graded cyclic trace

`boxed(Tr(sigma^k|M_f;t)=(1+t^p)/(1+t))`

for every `1<=k<=p-1`.

Taking `t=1` gives

### Corollary CCM.2 — exponential-to-constant collapse

`boxed(Tr(sigma^k|M_f)=1)`

for every nonidentity `sigma^k`, whereas

`dim M_f=2^(p-1)`.

Thus the cyclic trace of the exponentially large cubic Milnor algebra is exactly one.

## 4. Vanishing cohomology interpretation

For an isolated homogeneous hypersurface singularity, the Jacobian/Milnor algebra realizes the vanishing cohomology with its finite automorphism action, up to the standard cohomological shift, Tate twist and residue determinant character.

Here `det(sigma|H)=1`, because a p-cycle has positive sign for odd `p`. Therefore the ordinary cyclic trace on vanishing cohomology has absolute value one; its supertrace differs only by the predictable sign from concentration in degree `p-2`.

No estimate depending on the Milnor number is required.

## 5. Relation to the Fortune corner

After removal of the explicit affine Artin–Schreier class, the unresolved corner input is tame. The p-fold cyclic Thom–Sebastiani normal function has, on the additive-convolution hyperplane, the characteristic-p cancellation

`sum_i h_i^p=(sum_i h_i)^p=0`

and the surviving leading normal form is cubic. The theorem above computes the cyclic trace of the corresponding tame lift.

If the resolved tame augmentation specialization is identified functorially with this lifted cyclic cubic model, then each geometric sector contributes only a bounded cyclic local term even though its underlying Milnor number is exponential in `p`.

That identification is precisely the remaining `Tame cyclic lift specialization theorem`; it is not asserted here.

## 6. Why this is stronger than a sparse Airy fit

The theorem predicts bounded **cyclic trace**, not that the complete primitive Fourier trace function is one standard Airy or Kummer sheaf. A broad Fourier spectrum and failure of sparse Airy dictionaries are therefore compatible with CCM.1.
