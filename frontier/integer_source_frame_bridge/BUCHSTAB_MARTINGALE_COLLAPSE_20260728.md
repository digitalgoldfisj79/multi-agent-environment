# Buchstab-martingale collapse

Date: 28 July 2026  
Status: exact one-index martingale identity and complete-model quadratic variation proved; transfer to the primorial source grid open.

## 1. Tail notation

Let

\[
P=\prod_{p\le z}p,
\qquad
z<H<(z^+)^2,
\qquad
Y=\sqrt{P+H}.
\]

For primes `r` in `(H,Y]`, put

\[
v_r=\frac{r-2}{r-1},
\qquad
\xi_r(n)=\frac1{r-2}-\frac{r-1}{r-2}\mathbf1_{r\mid n}.
\]

Then

\[
v_r(1+\xi_r(n))=\mathbf1_{r\nmid n}.
\tag{1.1}
\]

Define

\[
V(H,Y)=\prod_{H<r\le Y}v_r,
\tag{1.2}
\]

\[
V[r,Y]=\prod_{r\le s\le Y\atop s\text{ prime}}v_s,
\tag{1.3}
\]

and the pre-`r` tail-roughness indicator

\[
R_{<r}(n)=
\prod_{H<s<r\atop s\text{ prime}}
\mathbf1_{s\nmid n}.
\tag{1.4}
\]

The empty product is one.

## 2. Exact ordered expansion

Order the tail primes as

\[
r_1<r_2<\cdots<r_K.
\]

The elementary telescoping identity

\[
\prod_{i=1}^K(1+x_i)
=
1+
\sum_{i=1}^K
x_i\prod_{h<i}(1+x_h)
\tag{2.1}
\]

applied with `x_i=xi_{r_i}(n)` gives the following.

### Theorem 2.1 (Buchstab-martingale identity)

\[
\boxed{
V(H,Y)
\prod_{H<r\le Y}(1+\xi_r(n))
=
V(H,Y)
+
\sum_{H<r\le Y}
V[r,Y]R_{<r}(n)\xi_r(n).
}
\tag{2.2}
\]

### Proof

Multiply (2.1) by `V(H,Y)`.  For the term indexed by `r_i`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
V(H,Y)
\prod_{h<i}(1+\xi_{r_h}(n))
&=
\prod_{h<i}v_{r_h}(1+\xi_{r_h}(n))
\prod_{h\ge i}v_{r_h}\\
&=
R_{<r_i}(n)V[r_i,Y]
\end{aligned}
\]

by (1.1).  \(\square\)

The complete higher-order Euler chaos has therefore collapsed to one ordered
prime index.

## 3. Exact prime detector after physical sieving

Let

\[
R_H(n)=
\prod_{z<r\le H}\mathbf1_{r\nmid n}.
\]

For a candidate prime offset `m`, every factor of `P+m` exceeds `z`.  Combining
the physical and tail products gives

### Corollary 3.1

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf1_{P+m\text{ prime}}
={}&R_H(P+m)V(H,Y)\\
&+
R_H(P+m)
\sum_{H<r\le Y}
V[r,Y]R_{<r}(P+m)\xi_r(P+m).
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{3.1}

This is exact.

The first line is the normalized `H`-rough coordinate.  The second line contains
one Buchstab increment for each possible least tail factor.

## 4. One-hit structure

Suppose `n=P+m` is `H`-rough.

If `n` is composite, let

\[
r_0=P^-(n).
\]

Then `H<r_0<=Y`.  For `r<r_0`,

\[
R_{<r}(n)=1,
\qquad
\xi_r(n)=\frac1{r-2}.
\]

At `r=r_0`,

\[
R_{<r_0}(n)=1,
\qquad
\xi_{r_0}(n)=-1.
\]

For every `r>r_0`,

\[
R_{<r}(n)=0.
\]

Thus exactly one negative hit increment occurs.  All earlier increments are small
baselines, and all later increments vanish.  If `n` is prime, every increment is
a baseline and the telescoping sum equals one.

This removes the exponential subset multiplicity of the unordered Euler-chaos
expansion.

## 5. Natural increment weights

For the logarithmically weighted prime detector, the increment at `r` has weight

\[
\boxed{
w_{P,m}(r)=\log(P+m)V[r,Y].}
\tag{5.1}

The factorization

\[
v_s=\left(1-\frac1s\right)
\left(1-\frac1{(s-1)^2}\right)
\]

and Mertens' theorem give, uniformly for `H<=r<=Y`,

\[
V[r,Y]
\asymp
\frac{\log r}{\log Y}.
\tag{5.2}

Since `log(P+m)=2log Y+o(1)`,

\[
\boxed{
w_{P,m}(r)\asymp\log r.}
\tag{5.3}

At the physical boundary `r=H`, the increment weight is `O(log X)`; it grows
smoothly to `O(X)` only for least factors near `Y`.

## 6. Complete-model martingale orthogonality

Place the tail residue coordinates on the product probability space in which each
offset residue is uniform on `mathbb F_r^*`, independently over tail primes.
Define

\[
D_r(n)=V[r,Y]R_{<r}(n)\xi_r(n).
\tag{6.1}

Let `mathcal F_r` be the sigma-algebra generated by the residue coordinates at
primes below or equal to `r`.  Then

\[
\mathbb E(D_r\mid\mathcal F_{r^-})=0
\tag{6.2}

because `R_{<r}` is measurable before `r` and `xi_r` has mean zero.  Therefore
the `D_r` are pairwise orthogonal martingale differences.

Moreover,

\[
\mathbb E|D_r|^2
=
V[r,Y]^2
V(H,r^-)
\frac1{r-2},
\tag{6.3}

where

\[
V(H,r^-)=\prod_{H<s<r}v_s
\]

is the probability of surviving the preceding tail primes.

## 7. Exact quadratic variation

The random variable

\[
I(n)=V(H,Y)
\prod_{H<r\le Y}(1+\xi_r(n))
\]

is the indicator that no tail prime divides `n`.  Under the complete product
model,

\[
\mathbb E I=V(H,Y)
\]

and

\[
\operatorname{Var}(I)
=V(H,Y)(1-V(H,Y)).
\]

By Theorem 2.1 and martingale orthogonality:

### Theorem 7.1

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{H<r\le Y}
V[r,Y]^2V(H,r^-)
\frac1{r-2}
=
V(H,Y)(1-V(H,Y)).
}
\tag{7.1}

This is an exact identity, not an asymptotic estimate.

For one logarithmically weighted candidate offset, the complete-model quadratic
variation is

\[
\boxed{
\log^2(P+m)V(H,Y)(1-V(H,Y)).
}
\tag{7.2}

Summed over `O(H/log H)` candidate offsets, (7.2) is `O(HX)`.

## 8. Correct transference target

The coefficient/quadratic-variation scale is already correct.  What remains is to
transfer the complete-model martingale orthogonality to the deterministic source
points

\[
P_j+m,
\qquad
j<N,
\qquad
m\in\mathcal P_{z_j}(H).
\]

A sufficient theorem is a centred martingale sampling inequality

\[
\boxed{
\sum_j
\left|
\sum_m\log(P_j+m)R_H(P_j+m)
\sum_{H<r\le Y_j}D_{j,r}(P_j+m)
-\mathfrak m_j
\right|^2
\ll NHX\,L(X),
}
\tag{8.1}

with `L(X)=o(log X)` and with `mathfrak m_j` the exact coherent principal
component inherited from the smooth-primitive centring.

The theorem must also retain the covariance with the normalized rough coordinate
in the first line of (3.1).

## 9. Technology boundary

Existing bilinear and trilinear Kloosterman-fraction estimates control fixed-depth
dyadic convolution forms and modest extensions beyond level one half.  The
physical first-order discrepancy occupies moduli from `H^{1/2}` to `H`, and the

identity above removes depth and coefficient growth, but a theorem transferring
martingale orthogonality across the primorial prefix orbit is not currently a
direct corollary of those results.
