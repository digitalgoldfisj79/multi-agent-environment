# Exact cubic-factorial sieve in the d=1 cubic slice

**Date:** 2026-07-21  
**Status:** all locally admissible cubic factorial moments are proved, signed and unsigned; cubic factors can be removed by finite exact inclusion-exclusion.

## 1. Setup

Let p >= 5, let a be nonzero in F_p, and put

`F_(a,c,d)(X)=X^p+aX^3+cX+d`.

On the locally admissible family define

`Q_(a,3,j) = sum_F binom(nu_3(F),j)`

and

`Q_(a,3,j)^chi = sum_F chi(Disc F) binom(nu_3(F),j)`.

Here `nu_3(F)` is the number of distinct monic irreducible cubic factors. Put `Q_(a,3,0)=(p^2-1)/3`, the exact locally admissible family size.

## 2. Generic marked-factor covers

The degree-eight compatible-orientation cover has geometric monodromy `S_8` by `CUBIC_MONODROMY.md`.

After marking one root in every compatible cubic block, the full geometric monodromy is

`C_3^8 semidirect S_8`

by `CUBIC_ROOT_MONODROMY.md`.

For every `1 <= j <= 8`, this group acts transitively on

- an ordered j-tuple of distinct cubic blocks; and
- one marked root in each selected block.

Therefore the corresponding ordered distinct j-factor marked cover is geometrically integral.

The finite-field form relevant to actual irreducible cubic factors is the canonical Frobenius-3-cycle twist of this cover. Twisting does not change geometric integrality. An F_p-point of the twisted cover is an ordered j-tuple of distinct compatible irreducible cubic factors together with one of their three roots in each block.

Lang-Weil therefore gives

`3^j j! M_(a,3,j) = p^2 + O(p^(3/2))`,

where `M_(a,3,j)` is the complete-slice factorial moment. Thus

`M_(a,3,j)=p^2/(j! 3^j)+O(p^(3/2))`

for `1 <= j <= 8`.

## 3. Independence of the local cubic

The local cubic is

`K_(a,c,d)(Z)=Z^3+((c+1)/a)Z+d/a`.

After geometric scaling it is enough to take a=1. Its generic Galois group over the coefficient plane is `S_3`, with discriminant

`Delta_H=-4(c+1)^3-27d^2`.

We show that its splitting field is linearly disjoint from the full marked-cubic splitting field.

The only nontrivial common quotient possible between `S_3` and

`C_3^8 semidirect S_8`

is `C_2`: the permutation module has only a trivial one-dimensional coinvariant quotient, so the `C_3` quotient combines with the sign quotient as `C_6`, not `S_3`.

The unique quadratic field in the marked-cubic extension is the sign field of the orientation `S_8` cover. At d=0, its square class is

`c^2-c+1`,

from the exact discriminant factorization in `CUBIC_MONODROMY.md`.

At d=0, the local discriminant has square class

`c+1`.

These classes are distinct over the algebraic closure. Hence the local `S_3` field is linearly disjoint from the marked-cubic field.

It follows that on every marked j-factor cover the local cubic retains full `S_3` monodromy. The proportion of Frobenius elements that are 3-cycles is `2/6=1/3`. Equivalently, the exact cubic irreducibility projector and fixed-degree Lang-Weil estimates give

`Q_(a,3,j) = (1/3)M_(a,3,j) + O(p^(3/2))`.

Therefore:

### Theorem CFS.1

For every `1 <= j <= 8`, uniformly in p and nonzero a,

`Q_(a,3,j)=p^2/(j! 3^(j+1))+O(p^(3/2))`.

All constants are absolute and effective.

## 4. Signed factorial moments

The exact degree-p discriminant character splits, after adjoining the square root that records `chi(-c/(3a))`, into the fixed raw Kummer classes already used in `CUBIC_LOCAL_INCIDENCE.md`.

For a=1 these may be represented by

`Fplus  = 4c^3+12c^2+9c+27d^2`,

`Fminus = 4c^3-12c^2+9c+27d^2`,

and their products with c.

The characteristic-zero audit in `oriented_cubic_symbolic_audit.py` proves that all required raw classes are generically nonsquare.

They also remain independent after adjoining both the marked-cubic closure and the local-cubic closure. Indeed, at d=0 their square classes are respectively c or 1, whereas the three nontrivial quadratic classes generated by the marked sign field and the local discriminant are

`c^2-c+1`, `c+1`, `(c^2-c+1)(c+1)`.

No raw degree-p discriminant class agrees with any of these classes. Consequently every signed Kummer sheaf remains nontrivial on all marked/local fibre powers.

Fixed-degree Lang-Weil estimates then give:

### Theorem CFS.2

For every `0 <= j <= 8`, uniformly in p and nonzero a,

`Q_(a,3,j)^chi = O(p^(3/2))`.

For j=0 this is the already proved locally admissible discriminant-mass estimate.

## 5. Exceptional orders

`CUBIC_FIBRE_DEGREE.md` proves the uniform bound

`nu_3(F) <= 24`.

The compatible map has generic degree eight. The coefficient pairs for which the algebraic fibre has more than eight points lie on the fixed discriminant/subresultant divisor. That divisor has `O(p)` rational points.

Therefore, for every `9 <= j <= 24`,

`Q_(a,3,j)=O(p)`,

`Q_(a,3,j)^chi=O(p)`.

## 6. Exact removal of every cubic factor

Let

`N_(a,no3) = # {locally admissible F : nu_3(F)=0}`

and

`M_(a,no3) = sum_(same family) chi(Disc F)`.

Since `nu_3 <= 24`, exact inclusion-exclusion gives

`N_(a,no3)=sum_(j=0)^24 (-1)^j Q_(a,3,j)`,

`M_(a,no3)=sum_(j=0)^24 (-1)^j Q_(a,3,j)^chi`.

Insert Theorems CFS.1 and CFS.2 and the exceptional-order bounds. Put

`C_3 = (1/3) sum_(j=0)^8 (-1/3)^j/j!`

`    = 189550849/793618560`.

### Theorem CFS.3

Uniformly for every p >= 5 and every nonzero a,

`N_(a,no3) = C_3 p^2 + O(p^(3/2))`,

`M_(a,no3) = O(p^(3/2))`.

Consequently

`N_(a,no3,+) = (C_3/2)p^2 + O(p^(3/2))`,

`N_(a,no3,-) = (C_3/2)p^2 + O(p^(3/2))`.

Numerically,

`C_3 = 0.23884377023642187...`,

which differs from `(1/3)e^(-1/3)` only beyond the eighth factorial term.

This is the second complete multiplicative deletion in the parity sieve. All simultaneous configurations of cubic factors are removed, including exceptional fibres with as many as 24 factors.

## 7. What this does and does not yet combine

The theorem removes cubic factors from the full locally admissible family. The quadratic theorem removes quadratic factors from the same family. To count members having neither quadratic nor cubic factors with the correct product main term requires mixed quadratic-cubic factorial moments.

Those mixed moments are now finite:

- quadratic order is at most three;
- cubic generic order is at most eight, with orders 9 through 24 confined to a divisor;
- every required cover has fixed dimension and degree.

The next sieve target is therefore a finite `4 x 9` mixed moment table, including discriminant weights, rather than an open-ended inclusion-exclusion problem.
