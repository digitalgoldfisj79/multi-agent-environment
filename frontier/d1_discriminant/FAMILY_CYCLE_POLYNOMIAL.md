# Family-averaged Frobenius cycle polynomial

**Date:** 2026-07-22  
**Status:** exact cycle-derivative identity and geometric reformulation proved; low-degree closed-form hypothesis refuted; wild-boundary cohomology target open.

## 1. Exact fibre identity

Let

`F_(a,c,d)=X^p+aX^3+cX+d`, `a!=0`,

and write its distinct monic irreducible factors as `h_1,...,h_r`, with degrees `d_1,...,d_r`. Define the reduced Frobenius cycle polynomial

`P_F(T)=product_(i=1)^r (1-T^(d_i)).`

This is the characteristic polynomial `det(1-T Phi)` of Frobenius acting on the geometric roots of the reduced fibre.

If F is irreducible, then `r=1`, `d_1=p`, and

`-P_F'(1)=p`.

If F has at least two distinct factors, then `P_F` has a zero of order at least two at `T=1`, so

`P_F'(1)=0`.

The only degree-p nonirreducible polynomial with one distinct factor is pure inseparable, `X^p-s`, which is impossible in the cubic slice because `a!=0`.

Therefore:

### Theorem FCP.1

`boxed(p 1_(F irreducible)=-P_F'(1).)`

Summing over the coefficient plane gives

`boxed(p N_a(p)=-d/dT [sum_(c,d) P_(F_(a,c,d))(T)]_(T=1).)`

This is the exact cycle-polynomial version of the Cartier and Berlekamp cofactor indicators.

## 2. Small-prime falsification scan

Define

`A_(p,a)(T)=sum_(c,d in F_p) P_(F_(a,c,d))(T)`

as an integer polynomial.

An exhaustive `python-flint` scan computed both square classes for every prime `p<=31`. It verified Theorem FCP.1 and found:

- `A_(p,a)(0)=p^2`;
- the coefficient of T is `-p^2`;
- the coefficients of `T^2` and `T^3` vanish throughout the tested range;
- from degree four onward the polynomial is already dense by `p=11`;
- no square-class-independent low-degree factor or stable sparse support appears.

Examples:

- `p=11`, square a: nonzero coefficients occur at degrees `0,1,4,5,6,7,8,9,10`;
- `p=13`, both classes: every degree from 4 through 13 is generally populated;
- by `p=29,31`, essentially the complete range 4 through p survives.

Thus the family-averaged cycle polynomial is an exact compression, but not a low-degree polynomial identity.

Hugging Face job: `6a60517913e6ef894d54af47`.

## 3. The p-cycle virtual representation

Let `P` be the p-dimensional permutation representation of `S_p` and let

`V=P-1`

be its standard representation. Put

`Lambda_p=sum_(i=0)^(p-1) (-1)^i exterior^i V`

in the rational representation ring.

For a permutation sigma,

`Tr(sigma|Lambda_p)=det(1-sigma|V)`.

If sigma has cycle lengths `d_1,...,d_r`, this determinant is the derivative-at-one cycle factor above. Hence:

### Theorem FCP.2

`Tr(sigma|Lambda_p)=p` if sigma is a p-cycle, and `0` otherwise.

The normalized virtual character

`rho_p=(1/p)Lambda_p`

is therefore exactly the indicator function of the p-cycle conjugacy class.

## 4. Geometric monodromy of the root cover

Consider the finite map

`pi_a:A^2_(x,c) -> A^2_(c,d)`,

`d=-x^p-a x^3-cx`.

Its generic fibre is the root set of `F_(a,c,d)`.

The generic polynomial is irreducible, so the geometric monodromy is transitive. Its degree is the prime p, hence the action is primitive. Along a generic finite critical point,

`3a x^2+c=0`,

the ramification is simple and contributes a transposition. A primitive subgroup of `S_p` containing a transposition is `S_p`. Therefore the geometric monodromy of the generic root cover is the full symmetric group.

The invariant multiplicity of `Lambda_p` is

`(1/|S_p|) sum_(sigma in S_p) Tr(sigma|Lambda_p)=1`,

because there are `(p-1)!` p-cycles. Thus the top compactly supported cohomology contributes the main term `p^2` to the unnormalized trace sum.

## 5. Cohomological crown target

Let U be the squarefree locus of the coefficient plane and let `L_a` be the virtual l-adic local system associated to `Lambda_p`. Then

`p N_a(p)=sum_((c,d) in U(F_p)) Tr(Frob_(c,d)|L_a)`.

Grothendieck-Lefschetz gives

`p N_a(p)=p^2 + E_a(p)`,

where `E_a(p)` is the alternating Frobenius trace on the lower compactly supported cohomology.

Consequently an absolute bound

`|E_a(p)| <= C p^(3/2)`

would imply

`N_a(p)=p+O(C sqrt(p))`

and hence positivity for every `p>C^2`, with the remaining primes already finite and machine-checkable.

The issue is not Deligne's weight bound; it is proving that the **virtual cohomological complexity** of `L_a` is bounded independently of p. Bounding the separate exterior-power sheaves gives exponential complexity and loses all cancellation.

## 6. Boundary localisation

The character of `Lambda_p` vanishes on every permutation that is not a p-cycle, including the identity.

Therefore, on any finite inertia subgroup containing no p-cycle, the restricted virtual representation is zero in the rational representation ring. Generic finite branching is generated by transpositions, so all ordinary finite branch contributions cancel in the virtual character.

At wild infinity, let `P=C_p` be the translation subgroup generated by a p-cycle. Then exactly

`boxed(Lambda_p|_(C_p)=p*1-Reg_(C_p).)`

Indeed both sides have trace zero at the identity and trace p at every nonidentity element of `C_p`.

Thus all nontrivial local complexity of the p-cycle indicator is concentrated at the wild boundary at infinity.

## 7. Exact local different and predicted inertia

For fixed c, write

`phi_c(x)=x^p+a x^3+c x`.

At infinity put `s=1/phi_c(x)` and `y=1/x`. Then

`s=y^p/(1+a y^(p-3)+c y^(p-1))`.

Differentiation in characteristic p gives

`v_y(ds/dy)=2p-4`.

Hence the different exponent of the original degree-p local extension is exactly `2p-4`.

If the Galois-closure inertia has the standard form

`C_p semidirect C_m`

with one lower jump j, the permutation-conductor identity forces

`j/m=(p-3)/(p-1)`.

The two integral possibilities are

- `m=p-1`, `j=p-3`;
- `m=(p-1)/2`, `j=(p-3)/2`.

The sparse cubic exponent pattern predicts the second case, but this identification is not yet promoted to theorem status here.

`x^p+a x^3+c x-t`,
