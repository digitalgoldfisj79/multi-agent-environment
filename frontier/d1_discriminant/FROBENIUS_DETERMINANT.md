# Reduced Frobenius determinant for the d=1 cubic slice

**Date:** 2026-07-21  
**Status:** exact irreducibility criterion proved.

## 1. Setup

Let p >= 5 be prime, let a be nonzero in F_p, and put

`F(X) = X^p + aX^3 + cX + d`.

Assume F is squarefree. This assumption holds automatically on the locally admissible set established in `DISCRIMINANT_MASS.md`.

Let

`A = F_p[X]/(F)`

and let Phi be the F_p-linear Frobenius operator

`Phi(z) = z^p`.

Use the ordered basis

`1, X, X^2, ..., X^(p-1)`

of A. Let B be the p by p matrix of `Phi - I` in this basis.

Because

`X^p = -aX^3 - cX - d` in A,

the j-th Frobenius column is obtained by reducing

`(-aX^3 - cX - d)^j`

modulo F. Thus B is a structured polynomial matrix in a,c,d.

## 2. Berlekamp fixed-space theorem

For squarefree F, the fixed subspace

`ker(Phi - I)`

has dimension equal to the number of monic irreducible factors of F.

Indeed, if

`A = product_i F_(p^(deg P_i))`

is the Chinese-remainder decomposition, the Frobenius-fixed elements are exactly one copy of F_p in each factor.

Consequently

`F is irreducible if and only if rank(B) = p - 1`.

The constant vector is always in the right kernel, so the first column of B is zero.

## 3. The correct left nullvector

The algebra trace

`Tr_A/F_p : A -> F_p`

is invariant under Frobenius. Therefore its coordinate row vector t satisfies

`t B = 0`.

A tempting but incorrect construction would delete the constant row. In characteristic p,

`Tr(1) = p = 0`,

so that cofactor vanishes even in the irreducible case.

The sparse coefficients provide a canonical nonzero trace coordinate instead.

### Lemma FD.1

`Tr(X^(p-3)) = 3a`.

### Proof

Let `s_k` be the k-th power sum of the p roots of F. Newton's identities give `s_k = 0` for `1 <= k < p-3`. At k = p-3,

`s_(p-3) + (p-3)a = 0`.

In F_p this is

`s_(p-3) = 3a`.

Since a is nonzero, this trace coordinate is nonzero.

## 4. Exact determinant criterion

Delete column 0 of B, corresponding to the constant basis vector, and delete row p-3, corresponding to the coefficient of `X^(p-3)`. Let the resulting `(p-1) by (p-1)` matrix be `B_red`, and define

`J_a(c,d) = det(B_red)`.

### Theorem FD.2

For every squarefree member of the slice,

`F(X) is irreducible over F_p if and only if J_a(c,d) != 0`.

### Proof

If F is reducible and squarefree, the Berlekamp fixed space has dimension at least two. Hence `rank(B) <= p-2`, so every `(p-1) by (p-1)` minor vanishes, including J_a.

Suppose F is irreducible. Then B has rank p-1. Its right kernel is spanned by the constant vector e_0, and its left kernel is spanned by the trace row t. For a rank-(p-1) matrix, the adjugate has rank one and is of the form

`adj(B) = lambda e_0 t`

for a nonzero scalar lambda. The cofactor obtained by deleting row p-3 and column 0 is, up to sign,

`lambda * t_(p-3) = lambda * 3a`,

which is nonzero. Therefore J_a(c,d) is nonzero.

## 5. Relation to the parity sieve

The parity sieve detects irreducibility by excluding all positive-parity factorizations with at least three factors. The Frobenius determinant packages all factor degrees at once:

`J_a(c,d) != 0`

is the exact full-cycle condition.

Equivalently, if sigma_F is Frobenius acting on the p roots, J_a detects whether sigma_F is a single p-cycle, whereas the discriminant character sees only the sign of sigma_F.

The determinant therefore avoids term-by-term inclusion-exclusion over factor degrees. Its cost is that its matrix size grows with p.

## 6. Structural features

The columns of B are reductions of powers of the cubic

`g(X) = -aX^3 - cX - d`.

Thus the determinant is not a generic p-dimensional determinant: it is generated by one cubic composition/power recurrence and the sparse reduction rule

`X^p -> g(X)`.

Immediate consequences include:

- `d = 0` forces J_a(c,d) = 0, because F then has the factor X;
- the involution `X -> -X` relates `(c,d)` to `(c,-d)`, so the canonical coefficient function is even in d up to a nonzero basis-change factor;
- affine changes of X act on J_a by nonzero determinant characters and preserve its zero locus.

## 7. Exact finite checks

The committed verifier constructs B directly over F_p and compares J_a(c,d) with an independent Rabin irreducibility test.

Exhaustive agreement holds for both square classes of a at

`p = 5, 7, 11, 13`.

The numbers of nonzero determinant values are respectively the known cubic-slice irreducible counts:

- p=5: 4 and 6;
- p=7: 10 and 8;
- p=11: 14 and 14;
- p=13: 10 and 6.

For p=5 and a=1, reducing J as a polynomial function on F_5^2 gives the exact factorization

`J_1(c,d) = 2 c^2 d^2 (c^2 - 1)(d^2 - 1)`.

For p=7 and a=1, the canonical function polynomial has the visible factors

`d^2(c + 1)`

times a residual polynomial. No stable general factorization has yet been established.

## 8. Next determinant experiment

For primes p through a computationally practical range:

1. construct the canonical function polynomial for J_a by reducing exponents modulo `T^p - T` in each coefficient variable;
2. factor its low-degree visible components;
3. compare those components with local-root, discriminant, and low-degree-factor loci;
4. search for a basis in which B_red becomes block triangular or a low-displacement-rank matrix;
5. test whether the residual determinant is a norm, resultant, or dynatomic factor.

A uniform triangularisation or explicit nonvanishing coefficient family would prove the d=1 theorem directly.
