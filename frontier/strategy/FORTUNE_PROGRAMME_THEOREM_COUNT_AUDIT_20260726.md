## Ruling

\[

**distinct unconditional theorem-level result units**.

This count includes positive structural theorems, exact identities, no-go/obstruction theorems, and exact computer-assisted finite theorems. It excludes:

- conjectures and open targets;
- empirical patterns and finite diagnostics not promoted to exact theorems;
- conditional implications whose hypotheses remain open;
- renamed or strengthened restatements of the same mathematical result;
- corrections and hostile-audit documents that do not introduce a new result;
- publication/build/reproducibility claims.

It does **not** mean that 82 results are confirmed new to the world literature. External novelty is a separate theorem-by-theorem question.

## Novelty boundary

There are three different questions:

1. **Generated and proved in this programme:** `82`, under the counting convention above.
2. **Plausibly new as stated:** a substantial subset, supported to varying degrees by targeted literature searches and the manuscript relation-to-literature sections.
3. **Confirmed globally novel:** no exact numerical count can yet be certified. Literature searches cannot prove absence, and most results have not had specialist external novelty review.

The strongest explicit repository-level novelty adjudication is currently attached to the quantised Kloosterman identity: classical method, result apparently new as stated. Paper I also records that its interval-specific integral Smith-form refinement was not located in the gain-graph literature. These are evidence, not a substitute for external specialist review.

## Bottom line

Use **82** when describing the number of distinct theorem-level results produced by the programme. Do **not** describe this as “82 new theorems in mathematics.” The externally defensible wording is:

> The programme has produced 82 distinct proved theorem-level results, including structural identities, obstruction theorems and exact computer-assisted results. A smaller subset appears genuinely novel, but the global novelty count remains unconfirmed pending theorem-by-theorem specialist review.
