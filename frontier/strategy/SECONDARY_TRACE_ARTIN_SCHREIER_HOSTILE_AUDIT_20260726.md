# Hostile audit: secondary trace and Artin–Schreier quotient

**Date:** 2026-07-26  
**Audited file:** `SECONDARY_HATTORI_STALLINGS_TRACE_AND_ARTIN_SCHREIER_QUOTIENT_20260726.md`  
**Verdict:** **PASS**, with the categorical and geometric scope restrictions below. The crown remains open.

## 1. Hattori–Stallings normalization

For a matrix over `Z[C_p]`, the Hattori–Stallings trace is the sum of the diagonal group-ring entries. On the regular lattice, multiplication by `sigma^j`, followed by `sigma^{-r}`, has ordinary trace `p` exactly when `j=r` and zero otherwise. Therefore

\[
\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbf Z}(\Phi\sigma^{-r})=p h_r
\]

is correctly normalized.

This statement applies directly to finite free or perfect group-ring models. The theorem does **not** by itself prove that every desired integral cohomology model is perfect over the ramified group ring. The point-level permutation-module and fixed-locus formulas used later are unconditional and do not depend on that unresolved categorical enhancement.

## 2. Freeness of the root-cycle action

For prime `p`, every nonidentity element of `C_p` generates the full cycle. A fixed ordered tuple is diagonal. On a diagonal tuple,

\[
e_{p-3}=\binom p3r^{p-3}=0
\]

in characteristic `p`, contradicting `a!=0`. This excludes the complete fixed scheme, not merely its rational points, because the fixed-locus equations plus `e_{p-3}-a` contain the nonzero constant `a`.

The affine invariant quotient is therefore a genuine constant-`C_p` torsor. Constant `C_p` is finite étale even in characteristic `p`; this must not be confused with the non-étale group scheme `alpha_p`.

## 3. Transfer and weighted difference

Every nonempty proper subset of `F_p` has a free translation orbit. The orbit sum of the chosen `(p-3)`-subset representatives is exactly `e_{p-3}`. For

\[
U=\sum_jj\sigma^j(t),
\]

reindexing gives

\[
(\sigma-1)U=-\sum_j\sigma^j(t)=-a.
\]

Thus `y=-U/a` has `sigma(y)-y=1`. No division by a variable occurs because the theorem is restricted to the fixed slice `a in F_p^*`.

The existence of such a `y` and equality of degrees show that the quotient torsor is represented by

\[
T^p-T=g,
\qquad g=y^p-y.
\]

## 4. Frobenius convention

The theorem uses point action `sigma(x_i)=x_{i+1}` and pullback `sigma^*y=y+1`. If

\[
F(x)=\sigma^r x,
\]

then

\[
y(x)^p=y(Fx)=y(\sigma^r x)=y(x)+r,
\]

so `g=r`. Replacing `sigma` by its inverse changes `r` to `-r` but leaves the nonzero-class and `g=1` conclusions equivalent after relabelling. There is no load-bearing sign ambiguity.

## 5. Irreducibility-section count

For `r!=0`, an `F sigma^{-r}`-fixed tuple is generated by one element whose degree divides `p`. Its degree is `1` or `p`. Degree `1` makes the tuple diagonal and contradicts `a!=0`; therefore it has degree `p` and its polynomial is irreducible.

For a fixed irreducible polynomial and fixed `r!=0`, there are exactly `p` compatible ordered tuples, one for each initial root. The free cyclic quotient identifies those `p` tuples. Hence there is exactly one quotient point per irreducible polynomial on each nonzero level. The identities

\[
N_a=\#C_{a,1}(F_p),
\qquad
M_a=\sum_{C_{a,1}(F_p)}c
\]

are exact.

## 6. Reciprocal formula

For odd `p`, the coefficient signs give

\[
e_{p-3}(x)=a,
\qquad e_p(x)=-d.
\]

On `d!=0`,

\[
e_3(x^{-1})=e_{p-3}(x)/e_p(x)=-a/d.
\]

The weighted triple transfer has difference `a/d`, so multiplication by `d/a` gives an Artin–Schreier coordinate with difference `1`. Every irreducible member has `d!=0`; the reciprocal formula therefore covers the complete target locus.

It does not lower the coefficient-space degree: multiplying a reciprocal triple monomial by `d=-\prod x_i` yields a complementary `(p-3)`-fold root monomial.

## 7. No-split theorem

If `f` splits over `F_p`, every distinct root is a root of

\[
h(X)=aX^3+(c+1)X+d,
\]

because `x^p=x` on `F_p`. Thus the squarefree support `R` has degree at most `3`.

The logarithmic derivative is

\[
f'/f=P/R.
\]

Here `P!=0` because `f'=3aX^2+c` is nonzero for `p>=5` and `a!=0`. Cross multiplication gives

\[
f'R=Pf.
\]

The left degree is at most `5`; the right degree is at least `p`. Therefore complete splitting is impossible for `p>5`. The two displayed `p=5` factorizations confirm sharpness.

The resulting formula

\[
\#Y_a(F_p)=(p-1)N_a
\]

is correct: the zero Artin–Schreier class is empty, and every one of the `p-1` nonzero classes contains exactly `N_a` points.

## 8. What generic trace-surjective theory does not provide

Trace-surjectivity correctly places the slice among modular Galois extensions. It does not imply that this particular invariant ring is polynomial, rational, s-projective or guaranteed to have an `F_p` point. Polynomial/retract conclusions in the general theory apply to special s-projective objects; trace-surjective algebras as a whole can have essentially arbitrary invariant rings.

Therefore it would be invalid to infer the crown merely from the existence of `t/a` with trace `1`.

## 9. Resolvent probe scope

The interpolation probe is exact for its finite primes and proves, for example, that at `p=29` the square class admits no total-degree-at-most-four relation in `(c,d^2)` vanishing on all target points. It also shows that the first relations generally contain extra rational points.

It does **not** prove that the geometric level scheme lacks a useful higher-degree model, a special compactification or a cohomological point theorem. Its ruling is limited to the naive bounded-degree plane-elimination shortcut.
