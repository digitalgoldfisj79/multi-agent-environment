# Fortune corpus Zeta23-style trust and reduction audit v1

## Purpose

Apply the useful parts of the Zeta23 methodology to authoritative Papers I–VI and the corrected integer synthesis without pretending that every paper has the same formalisation profile.

This is a bounded assurance/reduction audit. It does **not** reopen the integer Fortune mainline unless a paper exposes a genuinely weaker arithmetic theorem than the frozen selected-centre/four-prime frontier.

## Three gates per paper

1. **Independent statement gate.** Can the decisive claim be stated from Mathlib/basic definitions without importing the implementation? If an implementation theorem already exists, replay it through a literal bridge and inspect `#print axioms`.
2. **Finite-certificate gate.** Identify every claim whose only non-kernel input is finite exact computation/symbolic algebra and determine whether it can be converted into exact kernel-checkable certificate data.
3. **Reduction gate.** Test whether matrix compression, signed/inertia reformulation, geometric quotienting, or algebraic dualisation actually weakens the open theorem. A reformulation that leaves the same unknown count/covariance is a FAIL, not progress.

## Paper lanes

### Paper I — collision geometry

- Audit exact fourth-moment, collision, affine-rank/Smith and null-law claims.
- Determine whether any existing formal module proves a manuscript headline theorem.
- Do **not** rerun PSD stable-rank ideas already closed in the Paper VII transfer experiment.
- Reduction success requires a new signed/indefinite object with a provable low-inertia theorem that lowers the HTE4/centred rank-two analytic burden.

### Paper II — corrected prime detector

- Independently restate the one-failure block criterion and centred/four-prime residual identities.
- Bridge to `FortuneFormal.Integer.BlockCriterion` without importing analytic hypotheses.
- Audit the detector/source identities for hidden assumptions.
- Reduction success requires an arithmetic target strictly weaker than the selected-centre four-prime covariance / signed residual frontier.

### Paper III — pair-sum rigidity

- Separate finite combinatorial kernel theory from the arithmetic sampling theorem.
- Audit whether superincreasing rigidity/moment arguments are suitable for small trusted challenge statements.
- Reduction success requires a genuine sampling/transference theorem; a stronger Lebesgue tail alone is a FAIL.

### Paper IV — random order

- Audit statement/implementation separation and proof dependencies.
- Identify any finite configuration ledger that could be certificate-checked independently.
- Reduction success requires an increasing-order transfer; improved random-order moments alone are a FAIL.

### Paper V — function-field normal forms and q-line geometry

- Inventory exact computer-assisted census and symbolic claims.
- Separate finite census/certificate work from the open q-line non-saturation theorem.
- Test whether rank/inertia or quotient compression turns strict nonsaturation into a genuinely lower-strength statement.
- Reduction success requires a one-sided theorem not algebraically equivalent to cubic positivity.

### Paper VI — secondary traces / Kummer quotient

- Inventory exact algebraic/no-go/quotient claims and their computational dependencies.
- Identify finite identities suitable for reflected/kernel certificate checking.
- Reduction success requires a nonvanishing mechanism not equivalent to `W_p>0` or rational-point existence on the same Kummer open.

### Corrected synthesis

- Independently restate the exact finite implication chain already represented by the integer Lean modules.
- Confirm every analytic hypothesis remains explicit and no implementation axiom is imported.
- Reduction success requires a strictly weaker premise than the frozen arithmetic frontier.

## Cross-corpus no-go checks

1. PSD Gram compression cannot improve a one-failure stable-rank criterion at fixed row energies; this is already proved in the Zeta23 transfer closeout and is treated as inherited.
2. For a nonnegative target count `N>0`, a PSD trace certificate whose trace is proportional to `N` is not a reduction unless its Frobenius/inertia input is independently easier than estimating `N`.
3. Random-order averaging is not increasing-order derandomisation.
4. A quotient whose rational-point count is exactly affine-linear in `W_p` has not reduced the crown.
5. A finite certificate may remove computational trust but cannot replace a universal normalization/transference theorem.
