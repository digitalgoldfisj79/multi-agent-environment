# Fortune corpus — Zeta23-style trust and reduction assessment

## Executive result

`TRUST_AND_CERTIFICATE_GAINS__NO_FRONTIER_REDUCTION`

Applying the useful parts of the Zeta23 methodology across authoritative Papers I–VI and the corrected synthesis improves assurance and identifies additional kernelisation opportunities, but it does **not** supply a new theorem that weakens any of the three terminal mathematical frontiers.

Fortune's conjecture remains unproved. The integer mainline remains `CLOSED`.

The audit deliberately separates three questions which had previously been mixed too easily:

1. Can the decisive statement be written independently of its implementation?
2. Can external finite computation be converted into a reproducible or kernel-checkable certificate?
3. Does a new representation actually require a weaker theorem, or merely rename the same open target?

## Paper I — collision geometry

**Disposition:** `MANUSCRIPT_ONLY_NO_FINITE_BOUNDARY / NO_GAIN`.

Paper I's exact collision identities, divisor-incidence estimates, interval endpoint-graph rank/Smith classification and null covariance laws are appropriate candidates for selective formalisation. They are not, however, blocked by an external finite certificate comparable to Paper VII's former Singular boundary.

The open HTE4/HWF4/FBHE4/RQHE4 estimates remain genuinely analytic. Moreover, the corrected cross-paper audit already shows that even success there would not supply the signed bridge to the actual prime-pair detector. The inherited PSD finite-compression no-go rules out treating ordinary Gram stable rank as such a bridge.

**Best assurance target:** the interval endpoint-graph rank/Smith theorem, or the exact fourth-moment identity, as an independently stated Lean theorem.

## Paper II — corrected prime detector

**Disposition:** `TRUST_GAIN_ONLY / REFORMULATION_EQUIVALENT_TO_OPEN_TARGET`.

This paper gains the most from the trusted/untrusted statement split on the integer side. A new Mathlib-only challenge layer states:

- the one-failure variance criterion;
- the exact centred second-moment identity;
- the four-prime covariance substitution identity.

A separate bridge uses the existing `FortuneFormal.Integer.BlockCriterion` implementation. The comparator theorem is designed not to import the Paper VII project axiom.

This materially improves trust in the finite logical spine, but the paper's actual analytic gaps are unchanged: sparse-centre corrected first moment, source-to-frame transference, and block variance with loss `o(log X)`.

No matrix compression tested in the preceding Zeta23 transfer run reduces this arithmetic requirement.

## Paper III — pair-sum rigidity

**Disposition:** `TRUST_GAIN_ONLY / REFORMULATION_EQUIVALENT_TO_OPEN_TARGET`.

The corrected detector/covariance end of Paper III is now covered by the same independent statement layer. Its strongest additional assurance opportunity is not numerical: the bounded-coefficient rigidity lemma and resulting N-or-1 difference-multiplicity theorem are clean, finite combinatorial mathematics suitable for direct formalisation.

The Lebesgue exceptional-set and high-moment results remain valuable structural mathematics, but the audit reaffirms that they do not transfer to the arithmetic sampling measure without a new theorem. Strengthening the Lebesgue tail would therefore not constitute Fortune progress.

**Best next formal target:** bounded-coefficient rigidity followed by pair-sum difference multiplicity.

## Paper IV — random order

**Disposition:** `MANUSCRIPT_ONLY_NO_FINITE_BOUNDARY / NO_GAIN`.

Paper IV is fundamentally an analytic random-order theorem rather than a finite-certificate theorem. Its configuration ledger could be represented in machine-checkable data, but that would validate bookkeeping rather than close its main conceptual gap.

The decisive missing statement remains an increasing-order transfer, and there is still no bridge from the reciprocal random-order model to the corrected prime detector. Formalisation is therefore lower value than specialist human review of the logarithmic-slack argument.

## Paper V — function-field q-line geometry

**Disposition:** `FINITE_CERTIFICATE_OPPORTUNITY / REFORMULATION_EQUIVALENT_TO_OPEN_TARGET`.

This lane has substantial exact finite evidence that is amenable to the Zeta23 certificate philosophy. The independent reconstruction exhaustively checks the crown/orbit formulas at p=5,7,11 and the alternating-hook projector over cycle types; a separate C++ census verifies at p=7 that the sparse-cone singular locus is exactly the diagonal line.

The fresh rerun found one evidence-quality defect: the stored p=11 hook-character JSON had retained only the 11-cycle entry while the executable reconstruction checked every partition. The mathematical census did not fail; the artifact was incomplete and has been repaired.

This is a good candidate for reflected Lean certificates, but such kernelisation would improve trust only. The manuscript's own claim ledger makes strict q-line nonsaturation equivalent to the cubic positivity theorem. No quotient, rank or finite-compression representation in this audit weakens that universal nonvanishing problem.

## Paper VI — secondary traces and Kummer quotients

**Disposition:** `FINITE_CERTIFICATE_OPPORTUNITY / REFORMULATION_EQUIVALENT_TO_OPEN_TARGET`.

The independent reconstruction covers dual-number tangent identities, divided-hook multiplicities, Hattori–Stallings coefficient extraction regressions, finite no-split checks, Kummer cohomology-class/sign checks and exact quotient point-count identities. Fresh execution reproduces those values semantically; the first apparent mismatch was only JSON whitespace/pretty-print formatting.

Several of these exact identities are low-cost candidates for Lean kernelisation. But the essential mathematical boundary is unchanged: existence of a rational point on the Kummer open is another expression of the same one-sided cubic positivity / q-line nonsaturation target. The geometry has explanatory value without reducing the final theorem.

## Corrected synthesis

**Disposition:** `TRUST_GAIN_ONLY / REFORMULATION_EQUIVALENT_TO_OPEN_TARGET`.

The synthesis benefits from the independent integer challenge layer because the finite implication spine can now be stated without importing the implementation. Analytic hypotheses remain explicit rather than being hidden behind an implementation theorem.

The audit found no new premise below the frozen selected-centre signed/four-prime frontier. Thus the synthesis remains a precise map of what would suffice and why common absolute-value, low-order and random-order routes fail, not an unconditional proof programme.

## Cross-corpus conclusion

The Zeta23 methodology is highly useful as an **assurance discipline**, but its specific mathematical compression mechanism does not propagate automatically across this corpus.

The resulting hierarchy is:

- **Papers II–III + synthesis:** independent statement / implementation separation gives a real trust gain.
- **Papers V–VI:** finite exact evidence is reproducible and offers further kernel-certificate opportunities.
- **Paper I:** selected theorem formalisation would improve publication assurance, but there is no finite certificate boundary to eliminate.
- **Paper IV:** specialist proof review dominates formalisation in expected value.
- **No paper:** exposes a genuinely weaker open theorem than its existing terminal frontier.

Accordingly:

- Fortune: **not proved**;
- integer frontier: **closed to further reformulation-only work**;
- `INT-ISC` / equivalent signed selected-centre covariance: **open**;
- `D1-QLINE-NONSAT`: **open**;
- Paper VII datum-normalisation axiom: **separate, narrowed formal boundary from the preceding Zeta23 audit**.

## Recommended next assurance sequence

1. Formalise Paper III bounded-coefficient rigidity and the N-or-1 difference-multiplicity theorem.
2. Add independent challenge statements for Paper II candidate collapse and the exact source identity.
3. Reflect the most publication-relevant Paper V finite census/singularity certificates into Lean.
4. Kernelise the inexpensive exact Paper VI tangent/Kummer/point-count identities.
5. Send Paper IV to a human analytic-number-theory specialist rather than spending significant effort formalising its full analytic proof.

These steps increase confidence in the publishable corpus. None should be represented as progress toward Fortune unless it produces a new theorem crossing one of the explicit open frontiers.
